# Tardive Dyskinesia

Dopamine-blocker-associated tardive dyskinesia: recognition and VMAT2 inhibitor treatment.

## Features

Involuntary choreiform, athetoid, or stereotyped mouth, tongue, trunk, or limb movements persisting ≥4 weeks (docs/medical-docs/tardive-dyskinesia-criteria.md, Section: Diagnostic features). Exposure ≥3 months, or ≥1 month at age 60+; onset during exposure or within 4 weeks of oral or 8 weeks of LAI withdrawal (docs/medical-docs/tardive-dyskinesia-criteria.md, Section: Diagnostic features). Schooler-Kane probable TD: ≥3 months exposure, no alternative cause, plus moderate severity in one region or mild in ≥2; AIMS ≥3 in one region or ≥2 in two regions (docs/medical-docs/tardive-dyskinesia-criteria.md, Section: Research criteria and assessment). Exclude spontaneous dyskinesia, Huntington, Wilson, dental causes, and other drug-induced movements; distinguish from tremor, dystonia, and akathisia (docs/medical-docs/tardive-dyskinesia-criteria.md, Section: Differential diagnosis). AIMS supports measurement; total score alone does not establish diagnosis or determine intervention (docs/medical-docs/tardive-dyskinesia-criteria.md, Section: Research criteria and assessment).

## Treatment (APA Statement 14, grade 1B)

APA recommends a reversible VMAT2 inhibitor for moderate-severe or disabling TD; mild TD may be treated based on preference and impairment (docs/medical-docs/guideline/STATEMENT-14.md, Section: Implementation). Prefer deutetrabenazine/valbenazine over tetrabenazine for evidence base, half-life, and depression signal; Table 11 covers dosing, CYP2D6/3A4, QTc, and hepatic/renal limits (docs/medical-docs/guideline/STATEMENT-14.md, Section: TABLE 11). VMAT2 inhibitors reduce AIMS scores; harms are mainly sedation, with tetrabenazine adding EPS, depression, and akathisia concerns (docs/medical-docs/guideline/STATEMENT-14.md, Section: Harms). Anticholinergics do not help and may worsen TD; antipsychotic dose escalation only suppresses movements short-term (docs/medical-docs/guideline/STATEMENT-14.md, Section: Implementation).
